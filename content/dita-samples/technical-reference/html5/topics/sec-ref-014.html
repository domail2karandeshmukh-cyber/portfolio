Escalation matrix for security cases

Who the analyst on shift tells, how quickly, and through which channel, for each severity of case at Norrbyn Utilities. The matrix applies to every case the SOC opens, whatever raised it. Fictional sample written for this portfolio: the roles are real kinds of roles, and the people behind them aren't named.

Severities

SeverityDefinitionExamples
LowAn attempt that failed, from outside, against something that isn't exposed. No sign of success, no internal source.A port scan. A brute-force attempt against users that don't exist.
MediumAn attempt with a chance of success, or anything from an internal source until it's explained.A brute-force attempt against accounts that exist. Malware blocked on a workstation. An internal host probing another.
HighEvidence that an attacker got in, or that data left.A successful sign-in from an attacking source. Ransomware activity. Credentials found in a public place.
CriticalHigh, and the safety of people or the supply of water or power is affected or threatened.Access to a control-system network. A compromised engineering workstation that can reach field devices.

Who to tell, and when

The clock starts at the finding that set the severity, not at the alert.

SeverityTellWithinChannel
LowNobody now. Summarise in the shift handover.End of shiftShift log
MediumThe SOC lead, in the daily review. If found at night, the SOC lead by message.Next daily review, or one hour at nightCase comment with the Review tag; the SOC channel at night
HighThe incident lead on call.Fifteen minutesPhone call from the on-call rota, then the case number by message
CriticalThe incident lead on call, then the operations duty manager.Fifteen minutes for bothPhone calls. Don't rely on messages at this severity.

If the incident lead doesn't answer within ten minutes, call the next person on the rota. Record every call you make in the case, answered or not.

What to have ready when you call

  1. The case number.
  2. The severity and the finding that set it, in one sentence.
  3. The hosts and accounts involved.
  4. What you have already done, including any block.
  5. What you want decided, such as isolating a host, locking an account, or calling someone else in.

Who decides what

DecisionWho
Block an external address for 24 hoursThe analyst on shift
Block an internal addressThe SOC lead
Isolate a hostThe incident lead

Tell customers or the publicCommunications, on the head of security's request

Handover

A High or Critical case never changes hands without a conversation. At the end of a shift, the analyst walks the next analyst through the case timeline, and both add a handover comment. The incident lead stays the same until they hand over themselves.
